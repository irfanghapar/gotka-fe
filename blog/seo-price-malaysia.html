Ask three Malaysian agencies "what does SEO cost?" and it's common to get three very different answers, because "SEO" can mean anything from a one-off audit to a monthly content pipeline. Gotka's own SEO content plans start from RM900/month, rising to RM3,200/month for the most active tier, plus standalone articles for sites that don't want a subscription at all. This guide breaks down what actually drives that price, what each tier buys, and how to tell whether a retainer or a one-off article is the better starting point.

Why "SEO price" is really a content-production question

Search engines rank pages, not intentions — a business only shows up for a search term once a page exists that genuinely answers it, and answering it well takes research, writing and structuring time. Google's own guidance on ranking is explicit that content needs to be created for people first, not written mainly to attract search traffic, which is exactly why "cheap SEO" so often means thin, unhelpful pages that rank briefly and then fade (Google Search Central, Creating Helpful, Reliable, People-First Content). What Malaysian businesses are really paying for when they buy "SEO" is a recurring supply of pages worth ranking — and the price mostly tracks how much of that gets produced each month.

The three ways businesses actually pay for SEO content

In practice, Malaysian businesses cover SEO content one of three ways.

  • Do it in-house — no agency fee, but it needs someone who can research keywords, write well and understand basic on-page SEO, and that time still has a real cost even without an invoice attached.
  • Buy standalone articles — no monthly commitment, paying per piece only when new content is needed, which suits a site that just wants a few strong pages rather than an ongoing campaign.
  • A structured monthly retainer — a set publishing schedule billed monthly, aimed at businesses that want search visibility to keep compounding rather than arrive in one burst.

Gotka's SEO content retainer is the third option, built for businesses that would rather have a steady content pipeline running in the background than commission articles one at a time.

What SEO Starter, Growth and Accelerate actually include

Gotka's own SEO content retainer runs three tiers, billed monthly with no lock-in and no obligation to sign up at all:

  • SEO Starter — RM900/month: 2 new blog posts a month, basic keyword rank tracking, and a monthly traffic report.
  • SEO Growth — RM1,800/month: everything in Starter, plus 4 new posts a month, backlink outreach, competitor tracking, and a monthly strategy call.
  • SEO Accelerate — RM3,200/month: everything in Growth, plus 8 new posts a month, competitive gap analysis, custom graphics, and full GEO (AI search engine) content tuning.

A business that doesn't want a monthly plan at all can instead order standalone SEO-optimised articles from RM200 to RM350 per post (1,000–1,500 words), priced by length and research complexity — useful for a site that just needs a handful of strong pages rather than an ongoing content programme.

What actually moves the price

Within any tier or per-article quote, a few factors do most of the work in setting the price:

  • Publishing volume — the jump from 2 to 4 to 8 posts a month is the single biggest driver between SEO Starter, Growth and Accelerate.
  • Keyword research and competition — a term with several established pages already ranking for it takes more research and stronger content to compete for, which is part of what separates a RM200 article from a RM350 one.
  • Extras beyond writing — backlink outreach, competitor tracking and GEO/AI-search tuning are add-ons layered on top of plain content production, which is why Growth and Accelerate cost proportionally more than just "twice the posts".
  • Reporting and strategy involvement — a monthly strategy call and competitive gap analysis, as in Accelerate, add time beyond the articles themselves.

Google's own SEO guidance makes clear that ranking well depends on more than keywords on a page — site structure, descriptive titles, and content organised around what people are actually searching for all matter, which is why a proper SEO content service does more than fill a word count (Google Search Central, SEO Starter Guide).

Retainer, standalone article, or in-house — how to choose

A retainer earns its cost when a business wants search traffic to keep growing month after month — each new post adds another page that can rank, and the compounding effect is exactly what a one-off article can't deliver on its own. SEO Starter suits a site publishing its first regular content; Growth adds the outreach and competitor tracking a more contested niche needs; Accelerate suits a business chasing category-leading visibility and wants full GEO tuning so AI answer engines can also cite it, not just Google.

Standalone articles make more sense for a site that only needs a handful of strong pages — a new service page, a location page, or a couple of cornerstone guides — without committing to an ongoing schedule. In-house content can work well for a business with someone genuinely comfortable writing for search, but it's worth being honest that "free" in cash terms still costs real staff time, and that time competes with everything else that person is meant to be doing.

Other costs that continue whether or not there's an SEO retainer

SEO content isn't the only recurring line item a website carries. Hosting runs separately, from RM17.80/month on Gotka's Go AI Lite plan on a 36-month term, and a site that also wants ongoing edits, monitoring and small fixes can add Gotka's separate Care retainer, from RM150/month — a different service from SEO content, aimed at keeping the site running rather than growing its traffic. None of these are required alongside an SEO content plan; they're simply the other ongoing costs a website carries regardless of which content path is chosen. Questions and answers

How much does SEO content cost in Malaysia?

What’s included in an SEO content retainer?

A typical retainer covers keyword-researched blog posts published on a set monthly schedule, plus rank tracking and reporting.
