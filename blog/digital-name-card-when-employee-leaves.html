What Happens to a Digital Name Card When an Employee Leaves?

Gotka Technologies

Digital Name Card · 17 September 2026

Quick answer

By default, nothing — a digital name card keeps working after someone leaves until it is manually deactivated or reassigned, since it isn’t connected to payroll or an HR system. Contacts already saved from that card before it’s turned off are unaffected either way, because scanning or tapping saves a static copy straight into the recipient’s phone. The fix is treating deactivation as a standing step on the offboarding checklist, the same as disabling a company email account, and reassigning rather than deleting when a replacement is starting immediately in the same role.

A digital name card solves a problem paper cards never could — recalling one once it’s out in the world. But that only works if someone actually deactivates it when a person leaves, because nothing about a digital card does that on its own. What actually happens by default, what already-saved contacts keep regardless, and what a proper offboarding step looks like, are worth understanding before this becomes a live problem rather than a hypothetical one.

Nothing happens automatically — that’s the part worth knowing

A digital name card is a hosted profile page, reachable through a QR code or an NFC tap. It isn’t connected to payroll, an HR system, or an employee’s login status anywhere, so there is no resignation date that switches it off by itself. Unless someone with admin access deliberately deactivates or reassigns it, the card keeps working exactly as it did on the employee’s last working day — a QR code still resolves to the same page, and a physical NFC card still taps to it.

What a departed employee could still do with an active card

Until someone turns it off, a former employee’s digital name card still works precisely as intended: a scan or tap still saves their contact details, complete with the company’s name, logo and branding, to whoever they hand it to. Whether that’s used carelessly or simply because nobody remembered to switch it off, the practical risk is the same — new contacts keep being generated under a company’s name by someone who no longer represents it.

What happens to contacts already saved before that point

This is the part people most often get wrong: deactivating or editing a card after the fact does not reach back into anyone’s phone. Scanning or tapping a digital name card writes a one-time, static copy of the contact details straight into the recipient’s own contacts app, the same as if they’d typed it in by hand. Turning the card off afterwards only stops it from handing out the same details to anyone new — it has no effect on contacts that were saved earlier, correct or outdated.

Deactivate, delete, or reassign: picking the right one

  • Deactivate — the profile stops working but isn’t removed, so it can be restored if the situation changes, such as a resignation that’s withdrawn.
  • Delete — removes the card outright, appropriate once a role and its territory are genuinely closing, not being handed to someone else.
  • Reassign — swaps the departing employee’s name, photo and contact details for a replacement’s on the same card, keeping the same QR code and any printed NFC card working without reprinting — a natural fit for a sales territory or front-desk role a new hire steps straight into.

Making it part of the standard offboarding checklist

The most reliable fix is treating a digital name card the same way as a company email account or building access: something disabled on a specific, known date as a standing item on an offboarding checklist, not something remembered only if someone happens to think of it. Whoever runs HR or admin needs a clear trigger — a resignation form, an exit checklist, a message to whoever manages the company’s cards — the moment a departure date is confirmed.

Questions and answers

Does a digital name card automatically deactivate when an employee resigns?

No — unless someone actively turns it off or reassigns it, a digital name card keeps working exactly as before, because it's just a hosted profile page tied to a QR code or NFC tap, not something wired into payroll or an HR system that would switch it off on a resignation date. Deactivating it is a manual step that needs to be part of an offboarding checklist, the same way disabling a company email account is.

Can a former employee still hand out or use their old digital name card after leaving?

Yes, until someone deactivates it — the physical NFC card still taps to the same live profile, and the QR code still scans to it, regardless of whether the person still works there. This is the main risk worth planning for: a departed employee, intentionally or not, can keep sharing contact details that still carry the company's name and branding.

What happens to contacts that were already saved from a departing employee's card?

Those contacts don't update or disappear — scanning or tapping a digital name card saves a static copy of the details into the other person's phone at that moment, the same way a paper card's details would be. Deactivating or editing the card afterwards has no effect on contacts already saved; it only stops anyone new from getting the same (possibly outdated) details going forward.

Should a departing employee's card be deleted, deactivated, or reassigned?

It depends on the role. Deleting removes it outright; deactivating keeps the profile but stops it working, which is reversible if needed; reassigning swaps the person's name, photo and contact details onto the same card and QR code, which is useful for a role a replacement will step straight into, like a sales territory or a front-desk position, since the printed card and QR code don't need reprinting.

What should actually happen on a departing employee's last day?

The card should be deactivated (or reassigned, if a replacement is starting immediately) as part of the same checklist as disabling their email account and revoking building or system access — not left as an afterthought. Whoever admins the company's digital name cards should have a clear signal, like an offboarding form or a message from HR, that a specific card needs handling that day.

Can one admin manage and deactivate cards for an entire team from a single place?

Yes — on a shared plan covering multiple profiles, an admin can see, edit, reassign or deactivate any team member's card centrally, rather than relying on each employee to manage their own. This is what makes offboarding practical at any team size beyond one or two people.
