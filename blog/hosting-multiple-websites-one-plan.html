A web design agency juggling a dozen client sites, or a business running separate microsites for each product line or seasonal campaign, eventually asks the same question: does every one of these need its own hosting plan, or can they all live on one? Most cPanel-based hosting, including every Gotka Go AI plan, is genuinely built to run many separate websites from a single account — but understanding what that means technically, and where the real limits sit, matters before pointing a dozen domains at one login.

What "multiple websites on one hosting plan" actually means

A standard cPanel hosting account is not limited to the one domain it was set up with. Through a feature called addon domains, you can add extra, completely separate domain names to the same account — each one gets its own folder for files, its own database, its own email addresses, and, on Gotka's plans, its own free SSL certificate. A visitor to any of these sites sees a normal, independent website with no visible sign it shares an account with anything else.

What is shared is everything underneath the surface: the account's overall allocation of CPU processing power, RAM, and SSD storage. Every addon domain on the account draws from that same pool rather than having its own dedicated slice, which is the detail that matters most once you start deciding how many sites, and which sites, belong together.

Who this setup is genuinely built for

Running several separate websites from one plan is not a workaround or a compromise — for a specific set of situations, it is exactly the right tool:

  • Web design agencies managing client sites. Instead of a separate hosting bill and separate cPanel login for every client, an agency can run many client domains as addon domains on one account, with one invoice and one place to manage backups and updates.
  • A business running multiple brand or campaign microsites. A company promoting a product launch, a seasonal campaign, or a secondary brand under its own domain can host that microsite alongside its main site without opening a whole new hosting relationship for it.
  • Franchise or multi-location businesses. A business with several branches, each wanting its own domain for local search and local contact details, can host every location's site on one account instead of paying for hosting per outlet.

The real trade-off: one shared resource pool

This is the part worth being honest about upfront, because it is a genuine, structural limitation rather than a reason to avoid multi-site hosting altogether. Because every addon domain on an account shares the same CPU, RAM and storage pool, a website that suddenly gets far more traffic than usual — a viral post, a big promotion, a client's product going unexpectedly popular — can leave less capacity available for every other site on that same account, right at the moment performance matters most for everyone sharing it.

This is not a flaw specific to any one hosting provider; it is simply how shared resources work. It is entirely manageable, but it needs to be planned around rather than discovered the hard way during someone's busiest week.

How to plan around a shared resource pool

  • Check resource usage in cPanel regularly, not just when something feels slow, so a site's growing traffic is visible before it becomes a problem for its neighbours.
  • Group sites by traffic profile. Several quiet brochure-style sites can comfortably share one account; a site running e-commerce or expecting a heavy campaign is a better candidate for its own plan.
  • Keep higher-risk sites separate. A site handling online payments or sensitive customer data benefits from its own account as a security boundary, not just for performance reasons.
  • Back up and update each site individually even though they share an account, so one site's plugin issue or bad update cannot spill over into the others.

When to move to a dedicated plan instead

An agency or business does not need to abandon the multi-site model the moment one site grows — the first move is usually upgrading the whole account to a higher Go AI tier, which increases the size of the shared pool for everyone on it. A separate, dedicated plan for just that one site becomes the better option when its traffic or resource use is consistently large enough to compete with the other sites sharing the account, or when it needs a security or compliance boundary the others should not share, such as running online payments. Questions and answers

Can I host multiple separate websites on one hosting plan?

Yes. Most cPanel-based hosting, including every Gotka Go AI plan, lets you add extra domains to a single account as addon domains, each with its own folder, email and SSL certificate, so visitors see fully separate websites. Go AI Lite alone supports up to 30 websites on one account. All websites added this way still draw from that account's shared CPU, RAM and SSD storage pool.

What is an addon domain, and how is it different from a subdomain?

An addon domain is a completely separate, standalone domain name added to an existing hosting account, with its own folder, database and email addresses, so visitors see an independent website. A subdomain is instead a section of your existing domain, like shop.yourdomain.com. Addon domains are what let one hosting plan run genuinely separate brands or client sites; subdomains suit sections of a single site.

Will one busy website slow down the other sites on the same plan?

It can. Every website added to a hosting account through addon domains shares that account's CPU, RAM and storage pool rather than having dedicated resources of its own, so a sudden traffic spike on one site can leave less capacity for the others at the busiest moment. This is a genuine, structural trade-off of shared multi-site hosting, and it is manageable by watching usage and choosing enough headroom.

How many websites can I host on Gotka's hosting plans?

It depends on the tier. Go AI Lite allows up to 30 websites and 30 email accounts on 50GB SSD storage. Go AI Plus allows up to 100 websites with unlimited email on 150GB. Go AI Pro and Go AI Max allow unlimited websites and email, on 250GB and 400GB SSD respectively. All four run on LiteSpeed with cPanel, free SSL and daily backups.

Should a web design agency put every client site on one hosting account?

Many agencies do, since it keeps billing and management simple, but every client site then shares the same resource pool and the same cPanel login. A common middle ground is grouping sites by traffic level — quieter brochure sites together on one plan, and any client running e-commerce or heavy traffic on its own plan — so one client's spike or a compromised site cannot affect the others.

When should a website move off a shared multi-site plan onto its own plan?

The clearest signal is a site's traffic or resource use growing large enough that it is competing with the other sites on the same account, or needing a security boundary the others should not share, such as running e-commerce. At that point, moving that site to its own plan, or upgrading the whole account to a higher Go AI tier, restores headroom for everyone else.

Does each website on the same plan get its own SSL certificate and email?

Yes. On a cPanel-based plan, free SSL certificates and email accounts are issued per domain rather than per hosting account, so each addon domain gets its own certificate and its own set of email addresses that function independently of the other websites sharing the same plan.
